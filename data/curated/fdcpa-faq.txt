Debt Collector Contact: A debt collector may not contact you at inconvenient times or places. Generally, they cannot call before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. They also may not contact you at work if they know your employer disapproves.

Stopping Contact: You can stop a debt collector from contacting you by sending a written letter telling them to stop. After receiving your letter, the collector may only contact you to confirm they will stop or to tell you about a specific action (like filing a lawsuit).

Prohibited Conduct: Debt collectors cannot harass, oppress, or abuse you. They cannot use threats of violence, use obscene language, repeatedly call to annoy you, or publish your name on a list of people who refuse to pay debts.

False Statements: Debt collectors cannot lie or use deceptive practices. They cannot falsely claim to be attorneys or government representatives, falsely claim you committed a crime, misrepresent the amount you owe, or threaten actions they cannot legally take.

Your Rights: Within 5 days of first contacting you, a debt collector must send you a written notice telling you the amount of the debt, the name of the creditor, and that you have 30 days to dispute the debt.
