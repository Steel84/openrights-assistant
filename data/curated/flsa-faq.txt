Federal Minimum Wage: The current federal minimum wage is $7.25 per hour, effective July 24, 2009. Many states have minimum wages higher than the federal level. When both state and federal minimums apply, the worker is entitled to the higher wage.

Overtime Pay: The Fair Labor Standards Act requires that most employees receive overtime pay at a rate of one and one-half times their regular rate of pay for all hours worked over 40 in a workweek.

Exempt Employees: Certain employees may be exempt from overtime requirements, including executive, administrative, professional, and outside sales employees who meet specific salary and duties tests.

Child Labor: The FLSA restricts the hours and types of work that minors under 18 can perform. Children under 14 generally cannot be employed in non-agricultural jobs.

Record Keeping: Employers must keep accurate records of hours worked and wages paid. This includes the employee's name, address, date of birth, occupation, time and day the workweek begins, hours worked each day and week, regular hourly pay rate, total overtime earnings, and total wages paid each pay period.

Tipped Employees: Employers may pay tipped employees a cash wage of $2.13 per hour if the tips received bring the total compensation to at least $7.25 per hour.

Equal Pay: The Equal Pay Act, an amendment to the FLSA, requires that men and women receive equal pay for equal work in the same establishment.
