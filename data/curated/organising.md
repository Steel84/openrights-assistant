## Can I be fired for discussing my salary with coworkers?
Also asked: Can my employer stop me talking about pay? Is a pay secrecy rule legal? Can I be punished for sharing my wages?
For most private-sector employees, no. Talking with coworkers about pay is **protected concerted activity** under the National Labor Relations Act, and firing or disciplining you for it is unlawful.

A workplace rule or contract clause forbidding pay discussion is generally **unenforceable**, even if you signed it. You do not need a union for this protection.

It does not cover everyone. **Supervisors, independent contractors, agricultural and domestic workers, and most public-sector employees** fall outside the Act. Employees who learn pay information as part of their job, such as HR or payroll staff, may also be restricted.

Complaints go to the **National Labor Relations Board**, normally **within six months**.

## Can I be fired for organising or joining a union?
No. The Act protects your right to **form, join, or assist a union**, and to bargain collectively through representatives you choose.

An employer may not fire, demote, threaten, or interrogate you for it, nor promise benefits to talk you out of it.

## What is protected concerted activity?
Two or more employees acting together about **pay, hours, safety, or working conditions**. One employee acting on behalf of others counts too.

Raising a group complaint with management, discussing conditions with coworkers, and refusing unsafe work as a group are covered. A purely personal gripe about your own situation usually is not.

## Can I be fired for complaining about my working conditions?
If you raise it **together with coworkers**, or on their behalf, that is protected concerted activity and retaliation is unlawful.

Separate laws protect complaints about **safety** (OSHA), **unpaid wages** (Fair Labor Standards Act), and **discrimination** (Title VII). Each has its own deadline, and some are short.
