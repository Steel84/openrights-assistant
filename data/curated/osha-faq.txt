Right to a Safe Workplace: Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, every worker has the right to a safe workplace. Employers must provide a workplace free from recognized hazards that are causing or likely to cause death or serious physical harm.

Right to Refuse Dangerous Work: Workers have the right to refuse to do a job if they believe in good faith that they are exposed to an imminent danger. An imminent danger is any condition where there is reasonable expectation that death or serious physical harm could occur before the hazard can be eliminated.

Filing a Complaint: Workers can file a complaint with OSHA if they believe there is a serious hazard or their employer is not following OSHA standards. Workers may request an OSHA inspection of their workplace if they believe there is a serious hazard or violation of OSHA standards.

Retaliation Protection: It is illegal for an employer to fire, demote, transfer, or retaliate against a worker who files a safety complaint, reports an injury, or exercises any right under the Occupational Safety and Health Act.

Training: Employers must train workers about hazards in a language and vocabulary they can understand. Workers have the right to information about hazards in the workplace, including Material Safety Data Sheets.
