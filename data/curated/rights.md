## How do I file a legal complaint?
Also asked: make a complaint, report a violation, report a legal problem, where do I complain, file a claim
Start with the agency that enforces the law involved. **EEOC** handles employment discrimination, **OSHA** handles workplace safety, **NLRB** handles many labor-rights violations, **CFPB** handles many consumer-finance complaints, and **HUD** handles housing discrimination.

Use the agency's official website, describe what happened in plain language, include dates and names, and attach copies rather than originals. If you are unsure which agency applies, USA.gov's legal-aid directory is a useful starting point.

## What should I include in a legal complaint?
Also asked: what evidence do I need, what to write in a complaint, documents for a complaint, proof
Include **who was involved, what happened, when and where it happened, which rule or right may be involved, and what outcome you want**. Keep the account factual and chronological.

Save emails, messages, notices, contracts, pay records, photos, witness names, and confirmation numbers. Keep a copy of everything you submit and record the date you submitted it.

## How long do I have to file a legal complaint?
Also asked: complaint deadline, statute of limitations, how soon must I report, filing deadline
There is **no single deadline**. Deadlines depend on the law, the agency, the state, and the type of claim. Some federal complaint windows are short: for example, OSHA retaliation complaints can be due within 30 days, while many EEOC charges have 180- or 300-day limits.

Do not wait for an informal resolution if a deadline may be running. Check the enforcing agency's official deadline and get legal advice for a specific case.

## Can I be punished for filing a legal complaint?
Also asked: retaliation, fired for reporting, punished for complaining, retaliation at work
Retaliation can be unlawful when you report discrimination, unsafe work, unpaid wages, or another protected violation. The protection and filing deadline depend on the law.

Keep proof of the original report and any later change in treatment. A complaint is not a guarantee of a particular result, and this general information is not legal advice.

## Where can I get low-cost legal help?
Also asked: free lawyer, legal aid, pro bono attorney, help with a legal problem
Use **Legal Services Corporation** to find civil legal-aid organisations, or USA.gov's legal-aid directory. Law-school clinics, bar-association referral services, and nonprofit organisations may also help.

Tell the intake office your location, the deadline, and the type of problem. Legal-aid eligibility and availability vary, and criminal matters usually use a public defender rather than civil legal aid.
